5  Learning with AI: Gemini Notebook

5.1 What is Gemini Notebook?

Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is Google’s AI-powered research and learning assistant. A notebook is a workspace containing the sources you choose. You can ask questions about those sources and turn them into study materials such as summaries, study guides, mind maps, flashcards, quizzes, and Audio Overviews.

For this course, build your notebook from the learning materials linked from the HTML course book. Select the relevant sources and ask for answers with citations so you can check the explanations against those materials.

Source grounding makes an answer easier to check, but it does not make the answer automatically correct. Gemini Notebook can misunderstand a source, overlook a detail, or produce an inaccurate explanation. Use it as a study partner, not as an authority.

ImportantThe central rule

Do not ask AI to replace learning. Ask it to help you retrieve, explain, compare, practise, and verify what you are learning. Always open and read the cited part of the original course material before relying on an answer.

5.2 What you need

To use Gemini Notebook for this course, you need:

  • a personal Google Account or a university-managed Google Account;
  • access to Gemini Notebook enabled for that account;
  • a supported web browser and an internet connection;
  • the course materials you want to study; and
  • enough time to verify AI-generated answers against the original sources.

If you use a university-managed account and cannot open Gemini Notebook, access may need to be enabled by the institution’s administrator. Availability and individual features can also depend on age, account type, region, and subscription. Check the current requirements in the official Gemini Notebook help.

Gemini Notebook accepts sources including PDF, Markdown, Word, PowerPoint, CSV, images, pasted text, Google Docs, Google Slides, Google Sheets, web pages, public YouTube videos with captions, and audio files. The supported types and limits may change; the current list is available in the guide to adding sources.

WarningPrivacy and copyright

Upload only material you are allowed to use. Do not upload passwords, grades, Neptun identifiers, private correspondence, personal data about classmates, confidential research data, or copyrighted material that you are not permitted to share. Before using an institutional account, follow the university’s data-protection and acceptable-use rules.

5.3 Create a course notebook

5.3.1 1. Choose a useful scope

Start with one notebook named, for example, Computer Science 1 -- 2026/27/1. A single notebook is convenient for finding connections across the course. If it becomes difficult to navigate, create smaller notebooks for major topics, such as:

  • Files, RStudio, and Quarto;
  • Data and jamovi;
  • References and academic documents; and
  • Spreadsheets, Word, and presentations.

Smaller notebooks make it easier to select the relevant sources and reduce the chance that unrelated material influences an answer.

5.3.2 2. Add the learning materials linked from the HTML book

Open the syllabus in the HTML course book and follow the links for the topic you want to study. Add the actual learning materials at those destinations: laboratory explanations, practical tutorials, and relevant assignment instructions. For example, a notebook about data transformation could include:

  • Lab 06: Exploring Data;
  • Lab 07: Transforming Data; and
  • Homework 3.

For each material:

  1. Open its link in the published HTML book.
  2. Copy the full web address of the destination page from your browser.
  3. Open Gemini Notebook, choose the option to add a website source, and paste that address.
  4. Check the imported source to confirm that the relevant explanation or exercise is present and readable.

If a lab links to another tutorial or reference you need, open that link and add the destination as a separate source too. Adding the book’s home page or syllabus does not automatically import the materials linked from it. Use the HTML book as a guide to finding the sources for each topic.

Web imports may omit images or embedded content. Keep the original page open when studying screenshots, diagrams, or software instructions. If a page cannot be imported, copy the relevant text into a source when you have permission to do so, and include its title and original URL.

5.3.3 3. Name and organise the sources

Give each source a short, descriptive name, such as Syllabus, Lab 07 -- Transforming Data, or Homework 3. Before asking a question, select only the sources relevant to it. Mention source names in the prompt when you want a focused answer.

If the course book or a laboratory page changes, update the source. A web page or uploaded local file is generally a snapshot of its content at the time it was added. Google Drive sources can offer synchronisation, but you should still verify that Gemini Notebook is using the latest version.

5.4 A reliable learning workflow

Use the following cycle for each topic:

  1. Preview. Ask for a short map of the topic: key ideas, tools, prerequisites, and the order in which to learn them.
  2. Read. Study the relevant section of the book or lab yourself.
  3. Retrieve. Close the source and explain the topic from memory, or answer questions without assistance.
  4. Check. Ask Gemini Notebook to compare your explanation with the selected sources and identify omissions or misconceptions.
  5. Apply. Complete a new task in RStudio, jamovi, Word, a spreadsheet, or PowerPoint without copying an AI-generated final answer.
  6. Verify. Follow Gemini Notebook’s citations back to the course material and confirm every important claim or procedural step.
  7. Reflect. Record what you can now do independently and what still needs practice.

This cycle is more effective than repeatedly requesting summaries because it requires active recall and practical use of the material.

5.5 How to write useful prompts

A good prompt specifies five things:

  1. Role: what kind of help you want;
  2. Sources: which selected materials should be used;
  3. Task: what Gemini Notebook should do;
  4. Output: the desired structure and level; and
  5. Verification: how claims should be linked to the sources.

For example:

Act as a patient tutor for a first-year psychology student. Use only Lab 07 -- Transforming Data. Explain computed variables in plain language, give one new example, and end with three questions that test understanding. Cite the source for each procedural claim.

Specific prompts usually produce more useful results than requests such as “Explain this” or “Summarise everything.”

5.6 Examples for this course

5.6.1 Understand a new topic

Using Lab 01, explain the difference between a file, a directory, and a file extension. First use an everyday analogy, then give a technically accurate explanation. Finish with five short self-test questions. Do not show the answers until I ask for them.

5.6.2 Learn RStudio and Quarto

Use the RStudio and Quarto course materials. Create a checklist for starting a new Quarto project and rendering an HTML document. For each step, explain why it is necessary and cite the relevant passage.

If a render fails, do not ask Gemini Notebook merely to “fix it.” Paste the exact error message after removing personal paths or data, and ask:

Based only on the selected course sources, list the three most likely causes of this error. For each cause, give one safe diagnostic check. Separate statements supported by the sources from your own inference.

5.6.3 Practise data concepts and jamovi

Use Labs 05–07. Give me eight scenarios in which I must identify the variable type or choose an appropriate data transformation. Ask one question at a time. After each answer, tell me what was correct, what needs improvement, and where the relevant rule appears in the sources.

Compare renaming a variable, recoding levels, computing a new variable, transforming scores into categories, and filtering cases. Present the result as a table with purpose, input, output, and one example for each.

5.6.4 Prepare for a homework assignment

Use Homework 3 and the relevant jamovi labs. Turn the assignment into a sequence of checkpoints. Do not solve the assignment or choose a dataset for me. At each checkpoint, ask me to explain my decision before you give feedback.

This keeps ownership of the work with the student while still providing structure and formative feedback.

5.6.5 Improve bibliography management

Use the bibliography-management materials. Explain the workflow from a DOI to a checked bibliography entry and an in-text citation. Create a list of common metadata errors I should check manually in JabRef or Zotero.

5.6.6 Prepare a presentation

Use the presentation lab and the project instructions. Create a review rubric for my slides covering structure, readability, visual consistency, evidence, and timing. Ask me questions that help me evaluate my own work; do not write the presentation for me.

5.6.7 Plan study time

Use the syllabus and academic calendar. Make a two-week study plan for the next assignment. Include reading, active-recall questions, software practice, and a final verification step. Clearly distinguish official deadlines from the study dates you recommend.

Always confirm deadlines directly in the current syllabus and academic calendar. Do not rely on an older Gemini Notebook source or chat response for date-sensitive information.

5.7 Study materials in the Studio panel

Gemini Notebook can transform selected sources into several kinds of learning materials. Availability may vary by account.

Tool A productive use in this course What to check
Study guide or briefing Create an overview before reading a lab Does it include the practical steps, not only definitions?
Mind map See relationships among files, projects, data, analyses, and outputs Are the relationships actually supported by the sources?
Flashcards Practise terminology, menu locations, and concepts Can you answer before turning over the card?
Quiz Test understanding after studying Does the explanation justify the correct answer?
Audio Overview Review concepts while travelling or walking Recheck technical instructions visually before applying them

An Audio Overview can be customised by topic, level, language, and format. For example:

Create a concise Audio Overview for a beginner. Focus on the difference between data preparation and descriptive analysis in jamovi. Explain why each step matters, but do not invent menu commands not present in the selected labs.

Audio is useful for review, but it is not a substitute for operating the software. AI-generated audio can contain inaccuracies or glitches; Google describes the available options and limitations in the Audio Overview guide.

5.8 How to evaluate an AI answer

Before accepting an answer, ask:

  • Did the answer use the correct and current course sources?
  • Does every important claim have a citation?
  • Does the cited passage actually support the claim?
  • Can I reproduce the procedure in the relevant software?
  • Did the answer confuse an example with a general rule?
  • Did it invent a menu item, deadline, filename, or submission method?
  • Can I explain the answer in my own words without looking at it?

Useful follow-up prompts include:

Quote the shortest relevant passage for each step and explain how it supports your answer.

Which parts of your previous answer are direct statements from the sources, and which parts are inferences?

Is this question fully answered by the selected sources? If not, say what information is missing instead of guessing.

If the cited source and the answer disagree, trust the current course source and inform the instructor if the source itself appears inconsistent.

5.9 Academic integrity

Gemini Notebook may help you understand instructions, practise skills, generate self-test questions, review your reasoning, and find relevant passages. It should not complete assessed work on your behalf.

For homework and the project:

  • choose and inspect your own article or dataset;
  • make your own analytical and design decisions;
  • perform the work yourself in the required software;
  • verify every result against the data and course requirements;
  • write the submitted explanation in your own words; and
  • follow any AI-use rules given by the instructor.

Never submit AI-generated content that you do not understand or cannot reproduce. The aim of this course is not merely to produce files, but to develop skills you can use independently.

5.10 A 30-minute study session

Here is a simple routine you can reuse:

  1. 5 minutes: select one topic and the relevant sources.
  2. 5 minutes: ask for a concept map or three guiding questions.
  3. 10 minutes: study the original material and practise in the software.
  4. 5 minutes: answer a quiz or explain the procedure from memory.
  5. 5 minutes: check citations, correct mistakes, and write down the next step.

End the session with one sentence beginning: “I can now independently…” If you cannot complete that sentence honestly, the next session should include another practical attempt rather than another summary.

5.11 Final perspective

The most valuable feature of Gemini Notebook is not that it can produce a quick answer. Its value is that it can turn the linked course materials into an interactive learning environment: it can ask questions, connect ideas, adapt explanations, and guide you back to the relevant source. Used well, it shortens the distance between reading and practice while keeping the student responsible for verification, judgement, and the final work.
